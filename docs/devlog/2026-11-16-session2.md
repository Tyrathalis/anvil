### The user's question: is it an oversight that the gate cannot run offline?

- **It is a gap against a hard convention, not a deliberate trade.** "Seed everything; deterministic replay" holds for heuristic-seat games and for forks from a live state. It does not hold for a model-served game: the serving batch's numerics move leaf values in the third decimal, and sampling at temperature 0.025 flips on that. So seed, checkpoint and flags do not reproduce the game.
- **The patch is forced-decision replay.** The store already records every bridged decision's answer. A replay mode that feeds the recorded answers back, in place of asking a server, would reach any stored window exactly, with no model in the loop. The engine is seeded, so identical decisions should give an identical game. That last step is unverified for model-served games; the check is to replay about 20 stored games and compare winners and final states with `games.jsonl`.
- **What it would buy.** The gate on the big run's own 246,720 games. The charter's item 7, re-searching stored high-margin windows, which was never built for this reason. ADR-0125's truth stream on stored states. Drills mined from network-played games with a coordinate that outlives the process. Crash reproduction.
- **Its cost, as an estimate.** Fork work on the order of ADR-0117's certifier merge (about a day and a forkcheck), plus the fidelity read. ADR-0117 already routed a search front over a stored window by name and it was not built.
- **Routing.** A candidate for M13's first boundary; the user decides at the scoping. The overnight gate does not wait for it.

### The user's question: is the big run's full data worth keeping?

- **The ingested stores are worth keeping (105 GiB); most of the rest is not.**
  - The stores in `data/trajectories` are the games themselves. ADR-0126's trunk-capacity probe reads them, ADR-0125's critic trains on every store of the era, and forced-decision replay would make every window in them re-reachable.
  - The raw `workers/` output (275 GiB) duplicates the stores, pending a completeness check.
  - The per-iteration weights (168 GiB) are worth a thinned ladder: the cited iterations, every 25th, and the loop state. That frees about 161 GiB.
  - The in-loop arm reads (48 GiB) are the run's strength series. Their numbers are in the monitor rows; their games are low value.
- **Kopia is an undo window, not an archive.** Retention is about 48 hourly and 7 daily snapshots, then weeklies and monthlies. Deleted data is recoverable at full fidelity for about a week.
- **Proposed, for the user's sign-off:** after the ladder, check each ingested store against its raw source, then delete the raw `workers/` and thin the weights. About 435 GiB freed. Nothing is deleted yet.
