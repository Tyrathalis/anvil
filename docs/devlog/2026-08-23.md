# 2026-08-23 — the D4 control ran overnight: the veto-collapse prediction is falsified

Research track. `d6-run19` (run18 + the drill campaign restored) launched itself
through a chain at ~20:20 and halted on the veto guard at iteration 10 around
02:30. **ADR-0062's falsifiable prediction fails on both halves**
([ADR-0072](../decisions/ADR-0072-d4-control-run-veto-collapse-falsified.md)).
A second finding, turned up while answering a design question afterward,
outranks it.

## Done

- **The control ran and answered.** `d6-run19`, run18 verbatim + a 320-entry
  in-era drill campaign, 10 accepted iterations, GUARD HALT at i10
  (`veto_rate 0.3074 > 1.5× iter-0`). **No collapse:** kvr 0.0665 → 0.0640
  (−3.7%, CIs overlapping, validity 0.985–0.986) against run18's 0.0635 →
  0.0425 (−33%, CIs separated); veto slope i0–i7 **−0.00008 at t = −0.02**,
  squarely inside the drill-fed band, against run18's −0.00762 at t = −7.55.
  **No stability dividend:** halted at i10 against a HIGHER threshold (0.3044)
  than run17's (0.2925), one iteration EARLIER than the §3c-off run17.
  run18's decline was the missing campaign.
- **The taxonomy repeated the inverted signature under the opposite
  condition.** `knowable:timing` — which §3c cannot touch — fell −61.6%,
  hardest of any category, while `generic_short` ROSE 6.6% and `colors_short`
  moved −14.3%. Both runs now show the category §3c can't affect falling
  hardest. That is the discriminator, and it fires against the surface twice.
- **The capability negative replicated at double the head dose.** Pooled
  selectivity 1.19 (z = +1.13) vs run18's 1.14 (z = +0.75), with `kind_rms`
  reaching 0.0629 vs run18's 0.0337 on 836 traj/iteration vs 676. "Moved and
  didn't help", twice, at 2×.
- **The chain worked end to end.** Stock (1,999 games, 420 addressable, 46% of
  losses) → selection (420 candidates → 378 in-band → 320, ahead 0.1875, 42
  cross-store dups dropped) → run19, launched unattended. Two restoration
  facts got recorded because they bound the claim: run17's selection is not
  replayable post-boundary (the campaign re-simulates from seed on the current
  jar), and **the obvious free in-era stock was gate-poisoned** — the
  `m9-rebaselinearm` stores run at `seed_base 20260710`, `final_read.py`'s
  default, so curating drills from them would have contaminated D5's own
  strength read.
- **THE FINDING THAT OUTRANKS THE RUN.** Asked afterward what would make
  payment actually work, I went to check the certification semantics and found
  `payment_certify.py` scores arms on a **`HORIZON = 2` snapshot with
  board/tempo proxies** — life-preserved-and-creatures-kept, hand-down/board-up.
  **Not game outcomes.** The 69 certified positives establish a better board
  two turns later; whether that converts to winning has never been measured.
  An executor, an enumerator, a sub-head, an evalset and two training runs were
  spent against a ±1.1pp strength gate **without ever establishing that the
  taught behavior clears the gate's noise floor.** Two standing rules born:
  an evalset's certification horizon is part of its type, and measure the
  ceiling before building the learner.

## Broke / surprised me

- **I asserted the certified value was end-to-end verified, and it wasn't.**
  In the run19 read I wrote that the certified positives have "value measured
  end-to-end in real games under the current full stack" and used it to argue
  AGAINST the influence-surface hypothesis. That was wrong — HORIZON = 2. I
  found it one question later by reading `payment_certify.py` instead of
  reasoning from what I remembered about it. The correction inverts that
  argument: a 2-turn gain that doesn't survive to game end is *exactly* what
  "the value gets destroyed downstream" looks like, so the hypothesis I had
  just dismissed is the live one and now has a sharp test.
- **I nearly curated drills out of the gate-read seeds.** The
  `m9-rebaselinearm` stores were the obvious in-era stock — post-boundary,
  1,999 argmax games, free, already on disk. They run at `final_read.py`'s
  default seed base. Checking `run.json` before using them cost one command
  and saved D5's strength read.
- **The chain left its own watchd registration behind.** It handed off to
  run19 correctly, exited, and then showed as `DEAD ... state RUNNING` — the
  next tick would have alerted "job crashed" for a job that had COMPLETED,
  roughly at the user's bedtime. The stock wrapper had the self-unregister;
  the chain I wrote hours later didn't. Fixed in the script.
- **Two mid-run spikes both reversed.** i2 (0.2392) and i5 (0.2673) each
  looked like the signal arriving and each came back the next iteration. Not
  reading them was correct, and the run19 series ended as the second-noisiest
  in the ledger (resid sd 0.032). "No detectable decline" is the honest claim,
  not "proven flat".
- **`pos 5/64` at i9 was the best positive-drill score either run produced and
  meant nothing** — it came with 25 wrong auto-correct deviations (ratio 1.20,
  z = +0.51). The knob went up, not the discrimination. Exactly the error
  ADR-0069 charged D4's gate with, caught by applying ADR-0069's own rule.
