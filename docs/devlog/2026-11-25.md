## Done

- **Chronicle completion round, recorded in the sketch** ([collection-mode-sketch.md](../design/collection-mode-sketch.md), "Completion round" and the three sections after it). The weekly release cadence stays. The main dex is name-based with printings as a second layer. Universal card-for-card trading is rejected. The singles market opens early and thin. Played era tournaments pay one prize card per win on three daily tickets. The shop buys at half under a daily till, commons do not sell, and purchases are limited. Packs carry basic lands at the real rate and one copy unlocks unlimited use.
- **A simulation of the 1993-94 window** (`scripts/chronicle_window_sim.py`, modes `info`, `market`, `till`). Packs alone reach about two thirds of the 981 card names at tour pace. Three ticket wins a day make completion finite, about half a year. With a $5 daily till and one copy kept, 85% by day 70 and all names near day 107.
- **Period prices read from Scrye #4 (February 1995)**, the archive.org OCR text, one automated pass. Commons about 25 cents, uncommons about $1, weak rares about $3, a 30-to-1 spread among rares.
- **The Forge Web GUI has a phone layout.** MostCromulent's MVP PR (Card-Forge/forge#12906) is open and carries a portrait form and a web manifest. It still needs a Java host. Quest and Planar Conquest are ported on its development branch.
- The MVP plan gained a pointer to the round. The Now block's Chronicle bullet names it.

## Broke / surprised me

- The first market simulation looped forever. At period prices a booster resells above its cost at any sell ratio over about a quarter, so sale money bought packs without end. The daily till closes the loop; the discount does not.
- Forge's booster definitions for Alpha through Sixth Edition carry no basic lands. Basics have their own rarity and those sets are written as commons, uncommons and a rare. Quest hides it with free basics.
- A $1 average rare, derived from modern prices, was wrong for 1994 by a factor of three.
