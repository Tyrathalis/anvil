## Straggler tails in the big run's generation (night; the user's question)

- **Generation spent about a third of its wall time draining tails.** Each iteration ran three harness cells in sequence (240 searched self-play games, then two cells of 120 with a heuristic seat), and each cell waited for its last game. Read from the worker folders of all 1,545 cells (start = `cmd.txt`'s time, end = `games.jsonl`'s time): the searched cells' slots were idle 32% of their 389 hours, the heuristic-seat cells' 47% of their 322 hours. A cell had two or fewer workers active for 22% and 36% of its wall.
- **A continuous queue would have cut generation's 711 hours to between 437 and 495** (*estimate*). The low figure assumes a game takes the same time at any load. The high figure assumes the games that ran in a drained tail would take twice as long on a full box. The load effect itself is not measured: seconds per window by concurrency is confounded, because the games that run alone are the long ones.
- **The cause is cell size, not rare monsters.** A cell of 120 games on 24 slots is five games per slot in chunks of two, so its wall is set by its longest chunk. The longest 1% of games hold 6 to 8% of game-seconds.
- **Caps.** Of 123,598 searched games, 793 ended on the 900-second wall clock, 372 on the turn cap (52) and 176 on the window cap (1,650); 1,324 were draws in all (1.1%). The heuristic-seat cells: 302, 561 and 139. Of the 1,615 searched games over 900 seconds, 717 were won.
- **Not measured:** the read arms (their worker folders were thinned).
- **Routed to the M13 scoping (throughput):** one queue per iteration in place of three cells; carry-over of stragglers across iterations, with a control comparison before it enters the recipe; a cap raise priced after either. The wall clock is the one cap that depends on box load.
